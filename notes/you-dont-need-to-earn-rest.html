Notes · Newsletter — Issue 05

You don't need to earn rest

Your worth isn't dependent on productivity.

By Jess Mansell, LPC-MHSP · October 20, 2026

When was the last time you rested without first deciding you had earned it?

Not after finishing the list.

Not after hitting the goal.

Not after everyone else was taken care of.

Not because you were so exhausted that your body finally forced you to stop.

Just because you were allowed to.

For high achievers, this can be surprisingly difficult. Because productivity can become part of your identity.

You feel good when you're moving.

You feel valuable when you're contributing.

You feel safe when you're in control.

So when you stop, something inside you starts asking: Shouldn't I be doing something?

And suddenly you're lying on the couch while mentally reorganizing your entire life.

You call it relaxing. Your brain calls it a staff meeting. There is a deeper pattern underneath this.

When you've spent a long time measuring yourself by what you accomplish, stillness can feel like you're losing something.

Momentum.

Relevance.

Worth.

Control.

Who you are.

But your value does not increase because you had a productive Tuesday. And it doesn't decrease because you spent the afternoon doing absolutely nothing.

You are allowed to have a life that contains things that serve no measurable purpose.

Joy doesn't need an ROI.

Rest doesn't need a business case.

A slow morning doesn't need to make you more effective tomorrow.

Sometimes you get to experience your life simply because you are alive inside it.

And if that feels uncomfortable, pay attention. That discomfort may be showing you how tightly you've attached your worth to performance. You don't have to rip that identity away overnight.

Start smaller.

The next time you have an open hour, notice what you immediately want to fill it with. Then ask yourself: What would happen if I didn't?

Sit with that.

You may discover that what you're afraid of isn't actually wasting time.

You may be afraid of meeting yourself when you're no longer performing.

And that is exactly the place worth getting curious about.

Gut punch, am I right?

Take gentle care,
JM

Written by

Jess Mansell, LPC-MHSP

Founder, Anomaly Therapy. Jess works with executives, high performing professionals, and couples in Nashville.
