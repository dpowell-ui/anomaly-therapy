Notes · Newsletter — Issue 03

Is it anxiety, or is it intuition?

Your feelings can inform you without controlling you.

By Jess Mansell, LPC-MHSP · October 6, 2026

I want to give you a distinction that may change the way you listen to yourself. Because high performers are often incredibly intuitive, and they are also often incredibly anxious.

Those two voices can sound remarkably similar:

“Something feels off.”

“I have a bad feeling about this.”

“I don't know if I should do it.”

“Something is telling me to wait.”

So how do you know which one you're listening to?

Start by getting curious about the energy underneath it. Anxiety tends to create contraction. Our breathing quickens, our muscles tense, and our heart rate elevates.

I often tell clients, the antidote to anxiety is structure, reliability, and predictability.

Anxiety wants you to eliminate every possible risk before you move.

Intuition can be quieter. It doesn't necessarily feel comfortable, and sometimes the decision that is deeply aligned will still scare the hell out of you.

Fear does not automatically mean stop. That distinction matters because if you've spent years responding to anxiety by avoiding whatever triggered it, your nervous system can start treating expansion like danger.

A new opportunity feels dangerous.

Being visible feels dangerous.

Setting a boundary feels dangerous.

Saying what you actually want feels dangerous.

Choosing yourself feels dangerous.

And suddenly the safest thing is to stay exactly where you are. But safety and alignment are not always the same thing.

Here’s a question I want you to borrow: Am I in danger, or am I being expanded?

If you're actually in danger, tune in and listen because that's real.

Create safety.

Get support.

Reduce the scope.

Take care of yourself.

But if what you're experiencing is the discomfort of becoming someone new, don't automatically make the discomfort mean no. You may simply be meeting the edge of your current capacity. Man, I have been there and it is scary. Petrifying even.

And that edge gets wider through experience.

Through choosing.

Through moving.

Through discovering that you can do something that once felt impossible and remain completely intact.

You don't need to silence your anxiety. You need to stop handing it the microphone every time it gets loud. Your feelings can be information. They don't have to be instructions.

You have exactly what you need, and I'm here to remind you of that.

Take gentle care,
JM

Written by

Jess Mansell, LPC-MHSP
