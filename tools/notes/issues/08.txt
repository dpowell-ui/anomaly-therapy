Sometimes the hardest thing to admit is that you don't want what you used to want.

My gosh, what a scary thing to even think about, let alone say. We do this sort of work with clients daily. Whether it is an undercurrent in their conversation or direct statements riddled with discomfort and ambiguity.

It's scary because you worked hard for this.

You made sacrifices. You built the career.

You created the relationship.

You bought the house.

You became the person everyone said you should become.

And now something inside you is whispering: I think I want something different.

Immediately, the mind starts building a case against you.

But you've invested so much.

People will be disappointed.

This is a good life.

You should be grateful.

What if you regret it?

What if you're just bored?

What if you're self-sabotaging?

What if it doesn't work out?

What if it takes more than it gives?

So you stay.

And sometimes staying is the right choice. But sometimes you're staying because you don't know how to give yourself permission to evolve.

You are allowed to outgrow a version of your life.

You are allowed to change your mind.

You are allowed to discover that the thing you once desperately wanted no longer feels like the thing you want now.

That doesn't mean you failed. It means you're alive.

You're changing, and your vision is allowed to change with you.

This is where I want you to get very honest with yourself.

Are you protecting something because you genuinely still want it? Or are you protecting it because you don't want to admit that you've changed?

Those are different.

One is commitment. The other is attachment. And attachment can look incredibly responsible from the outside.

You can spend years maintaining something simply because you don't want to waste the years you already spent building it. But your past investment does not get to dictate your future.

You don't have to burn your life down.

You don't need a five-year plan.

You don't even need to know exactly what's next.

You can simply tell yourself the truth about what is true now.

What do you want more of?

What do you want less of?

What no longer fits?

What have you been afraid to admit?

Start there.

Because sometimes the next version of your life doesn't arrive with a grand plan.

Sometimes it starts with one quiet sentence: “I don't think I want this anymore.”

Even if that is a whisper, let yourself listen.

Take gentle care,

JM
