Have you ever had someone tell you one thing, then behave in a way that left you questioning the whole conversation? Suddenly you’re spending more energy trying to make sense of the relationship than actually experiencing it. It is so confusing.

You replay conversations. You question your interpretation. You wonder if you’re expecting too much. I want you to pay attention to that.

Safety grows when someone’s words and behavior match.

That’s congruency. It’s when what someone says is reflected in how they show up. Their actions support their words, and their commitments mean something because you see them follow through. Over time, that consistency gives you something to trust.

People are going to disappoint you, and you’re going to disappoint them. One moment doesn’t always tell you everything you need to know about someone.

What matters is the pattern.

Can they acknowledge when something doesn’t match? Do they take responsibility and try to repair it? Or do you keep having the same conversation, or taking ownership for something that isn’t yours to own?

When someone’s words and behavior repeatedly don’t match, you can start disconnecting from your own experience. You tell yourself you’re overthinking. You find reasons to excuse what keeps happening. You focus on what they meant instead of what their behavior keeps showing you.

Pause there.

You can understand why someone behaves a certain way and still acknowledge the impact it has on you. You don’t need to assume the worst about someone. You also don’t need to keep dismissing a pattern just because you can explain it.

Ask yourself: Are you paying attention to what someone consistently shows you, or holding on to what they’ve told you?

Self-trust grows when you stop dismissing what you can see, feel, and experience.

This week, a lot of us will sit across the table from people whose words and actions have never quite matched. You don’t have to fix that on Thursday. You don’t have to name it, either. Just notice what matches, what doesn’t, and whether there’s a willingness to repair the gap.

That is useful information.

And if this week feels heavy, that’s allowed too.
